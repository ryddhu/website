The problem

Safe-looking apps still hand children the open internet.

A child taps one suggestion tile and they're somewhere nobody vetted. Autoplay queues up whatever holds attention longest. And nearly every safety filter on the market reads English — while most of what an Indian child is shown is not in English.

One tap and they're gone

Suggested videos, channel links and end screens are all exits. A four-year-old doesn't know they've left the safe place.

Built to not be stopped

Infinite feeds and autoplay are engineered to hold attention. That's the opposite of what a small child needs.

Filters that only read English

Tamil, Hindi, Bengali, Telugu — written in native script or typed in Roman letters. Most filters see none of it.

How it works

Five layers, one score, your threshold.

Every video gets a safety score before it can appear. You choose how high that score has to be — and you can change it whenever you like.

1 · Words

Title, description and tags are matched against a blocklist you can edit — remove ours, add your own. Obfuscation like g.h.o.s.t is unpicked before matching.

2 · Language

15 Indian languages in native script and romanised spelling — because a parent in Chennai needs Tamil covered, not just English.

3 · Behaviour

Content that pushes children to copy dangerous stunts, watch compulsively, or respond to manufactured distress. Conventional filters look straight past this.

4 · Channel

Publisher reputation and history. Unknown channels are structurally capped — they can't reach the top band no matter how clean the title looks.

5 · Thumbnail

On-device image analysis reads text inside the thumbnail and measures how dark it is. The image never leaves your phone.

= Your threshold

The five layers combine into one score. You set the bar — strict for a four-year-old, looser for a nine-year-old. It's your call, not ours.

Privacy

We don't collect your child's data.
Not carefully — at all.

The industry standard is to collect data and promise to handle it well. We think that's the wrong shape of promise. There is no collection mechanism to misuse.

What stays on your phone

  • Watch history — encrypted with AES-256
  • Your settings and blocked words
  • Parent PIN — stored only as a hash
  • Video cache, cleared after 30 days

What we never collect

  • Name, age, photo or gender
  • Email, phone or location
  • Advertising ID or device fingerprint
  • Analytics of any kind — there is no SDK

Uninstall deletes everything

Because we hold no server-side copy, deleting the app deletes the data. There's no retention window and no backup to purge. Android backup is disabled so your child's history doesn't travel to Google Drive or to a new phone either. Read the full policy →

Honest limits

What Nubbito cannot do.

Please read this before you rely on it. A parent who over-trusts a filter is worse off than one who was never given it.

It can't watch the video

Nubbito reads titles, descriptions, tags, channels and thumbnails. It does not analyse video frames or audio. A video that looks fine and turns unsuitable partway through can pass the filter.

It can't screen ads

Video plays through YouTube, and YouTube may show advertisements. Those come from Google, not from us, and our filter cannot inspect them.

It isn't a substitute for you

No automated filter is perfect. Nubbito substantially reduces risk — it does not remove it. Please stay in the room.

Questions

Frequently asked.

Is Nubbito free?

Nubbito is in development and hasn't launched, so pricing isn't settled. What we can commit to now: we will never fund it by collecting or selling data about your child. That option is off the table permanently.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up, no login, and no password to remember. You set a parent PIN on the device during setup, and that's it.

Where do the videos come from?

YouTube. Nubbito uses the YouTube API Services to fetch videos from a curated set of vetted children's channels, then applies its own safety assessment before anything is shown. Video content remains subject to YouTube's terms.

Can my child get out of the app into YouTube?

Preventing exactly that is a core design goal. Playback is contained, and the app doesn't offer a route into open YouTube. Nothing is unbreakable — but a child tapping around will not find an exit.

What happens when my child hits the watch limit?

Playback stops with a gentle break message. Limits are off by default — you turn them on and choose the length, because a limit you didn't choose is one you'll resent.

Does the filter work if my child searches in Tamil?

Yes. That's a substantial part of why Nubbito exists. It matches both native script and romanised spelling — தமிழ் and tamil typed in Roman letters both work.

Which Android versions are supported?

Android 11 and above. Encryption relies on the Android Keystore, which is meaningfully stronger on recent versions.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet. Android first, since that's the majority of the Indian market. If Nubbito finds an audience, iOS follows.

Want to know when it's ready?

Nubbito is in development. Send us a line and we'll tell you when it launches — no marketing list, no drip campaign, just one email when there's something to try.
